01

Summary of findings

Hover, tap or focus a redaction to read it. Or flip Declassify all in the header.

The subject came to security from the hardware side: railway axle-counter sensors, PCB and IC floor planning, and a string of IoT builds that talk to the physical world. That background shows in how he approaches a system. He wants to know where the signal enters, which component trusts which, and what happens when one of them lies.

Since 2024 the work has been on the other side of the boundary: standardising security rule sets for financial institutions at Aujas, streamlining the NCA ECC, NCA CSCC and CCC frameworks, and testing web applications the way an attacker would.

He is now completing an M.S. in Cybersecurity at Stevens Institute of Technology in Hoboken, with a focus on security fundamentals, networking and applied cybersecurity. Current interests: compliance and regulatory frameworks, web application penetration testing, and securing IoT and embedded systems.

How this file is hardened

  • No third-party resources. Fonts, styles and scripts are served from this origin, so there is nothing to tamper with upstream and no one to leak your visit to.
  • Strict Content Security Policy, sent as a header and repeated in a <meta> tag. One inline script, allowed by hash, sets your theme before paint. No inline event handlers anywhere.
  • Trusted Types required for scripts: the page's own JavaScript never builds HTML from strings.
  • Every outbound link carries rel="noopener noreferrer", and the referrer policy is no-referrer.
  • No analytics, no cookies, no tracking pixels. Your theme choice is kept in your own browser's local storage and nowhere else.
  • Contact form carries a honeypot field that humans never see. Email copy uses a fixed string, never page content.
  • Works with JavaScript disabled: redactions, the theme that follows your system, the index, the form and every link.
  • Vulnerability disclosure path published in security.txt and the policy page.
